# Hi, I'm Vasavi Nallur Srinivasaraghavan 👋

**Stem cell biologist modeling neurological disorders with human iPSC-derived neurons and 3D cortical organoids.**

I study how genetic variants disrupt early brain development, with a focus on developmental *timing*: when gene expression changes, when cells migrate, and when circuits come online. My work has moved from rodent neurotoxicology to iPSC-derived neurons and then to patient-derived organoids, always toward more human-relevant models.

## 🧠 Highlights

- **First-author methods paper:** a customizable, cost-efficient qPCR array for screening iPSC-to-cortical-neuron differentiation efficiency.
- **Patient-derived organoid phenotyping:** in familial bipolar disorder, patient-derived neural stem cells showed reduced migration speed and directional persistence (n = 4 lines: 2 control, 2 patient; p < 0.0001). Patient organoids also showed progenitor proliferation and organization defects.
- **Isogenic disease modeling:** contributed to a CRISPR/Cas9-edited *SNCA* dosage series for Parkinson's disease research.

## 🛠️ Skills & methods

- **Stem cell culture:** human iPSC maintenance, NGN2-directed cortical neuron differentiation, embryoid bodies, cortical organoids (long-term), neural stem cells
- **Molecular biology:** SYBR Green qPCR assay and primer design, RT-PCR, gene expression profiling, CRISPR/Cas9 gene-edited line characterization
- **Imaging & analysis:** immunocytochemistry, live-cell time-lapse imaging, single-cell tracking and migration analysis, histology
- **In vivo & behavior:** rodent models, behavioral assays, antioxidant and oxidative stress biochemistry

## 🎯 Research interests

- Human 3D neural models (organoids and assembloids) of neurodevelopmental disorders
- Thalamus-subplate-cortex circuit assembly and its species-specific timing
- Critical windows of environmental risk exposure (e.g. valproic acid) in autism-associated models
- Whether the cell-intrinsic developmental clock stays in sync across brain compartments when high-confidence ASD genes (e.g. *CHD8*) are disrupted
